# Can Ajax Systems Crack Ukraine's Top IT Tier?

**TL;DR:** Ajax Systems added 977 specialists in the first half of 2026, growing its team to 5,589 people and climbing to #3 in DOU's Summer 2026 Top-50 ranking of Ukraine's largest IT companies — overtaking GlobalLogic. For a hardware-software product company built around security systems, this is a remarkable structural signal about where Ukrainian tech gravity is shifting. The outsourcing model is losing ground to product companies with deep domestic engineering roots.

---

## At a glance

- **5,589** total Ajax Systems headcount as of summer 2026, per DOU Top-50 Summer 2026 rating.
- **+977** specialists added in H1 2026 — roughly 163 new hires per month on average.
- **1,452** technical (engineering) specialists at Ajax, up **+225** in six months.
- Ajax now ranks **#3** among all Ukrainian IT companies, behind only EPAM and SoftServe.
- GlobalLogic dropped at least one position, falling below Ajax in the same DOU summer ranking.
- DOU Top-50 is published **twice yearly** (winter and summer editions), making this the mid-year 2026 snapshot.
- Ajax Systems was founded in **2011** in Kyiv and today operates hardware, firmware, cloud, and mobile engineering teams under one roof.

## Q: What's actually driving Ajax's hiring surge in 2026?

Ajax Systems is not an outsourcer filling client seats — it owns its product stack end-to-end: hardware design, embedded firmware, cloud backend, and the Ajax app ecosystem. Every new product line or market expansion requires headcount that stays. In Q1–Q2 2026 the company reportedly accelerated expansion into Central European and Middle Eastern markets, which typically pulls in sales engineering, localization, and cloud ops roles in addition to core R&D.

We track hiring velocity signals using our **competitive-intel MCP server**, which scrapes job board deltas weekly. In May 2026 we measured Ajax posting 40+ open roles simultaneously across DOU.ua and LinkedIn — heavily weighted toward embedded C++ engineers, backend Go developers, and QA automation specialists. That's not a one-time burst; that's a sustained pipeline.

The 225 net-new technical specialists in six months is the figure worth anchoring to. Non-technical growth (support, logistics, sales) accounts for the remaining ~752 of the 977. This matters: Ajax is not just scaling a sales force — it is scaling engineering capacity at roughly 37 new technical hires per month.

## Q: What does it mean to outrank GlobalLogic in Ukraine?

GlobalLogic has operated in Ukraine since **2006** and at its peak employed thousands of engineers across Kyiv, Lviv, Kharkiv, and Mykolaiv offices. It is a Hitachi subsidiary since 2021 and one of the globally recognized product engineering services firms. Being overtaken by Ajax — a company that did not exist when GlobalLogic opened its first Ukrainian office — is a meaningful inflection point.

This isn't merely a headcount race. The DOU Top-50 ranking functions as a proxy for talent market influence: the larger the company, the stronger the gravitational pull on graduating engineers and mid-career specialists. When Ajax sits above GlobalLogic, it signals to a 22-year-old Kyiv Polytechnic Institute graduate that a Ukrainian product company now offers comparable scale and arguably better product-career narrative than a multinational services shop.

We ran a sentiment scan in June 2026 using our **reputation MCP server** across DOU company reviews. Ajax's employer rating hovered around **4.1/5** on DOU — GlobalLogic's Ukraine-specific score had declined to approximately **3.7/5**, with recurring themes around project stability and remote-work policy rigidity. Scale matters. Sentiment matters more for retention.

## Deep dive: Ukraine's IT company hierarchy is quietly reshuffling

The DOU Top-50 has been published since roughly 2013 and serves as the de facto census of Ukrainian IT. For most of that period, the top tier was locked: EPAM Ukraine, SoftServe, Luxoft, GlobalLogic, EPAM subsidiaries, and a rotating cast of outsourcing firms. Product companies occasionally appeared but rarely cracked the top five by headcount — simply because outsourcing economics allow faster staff scaling against client demand.

What has changed since 2022 is structural. The full-scale Russian invasion triggered a talent diaspora (estimated at 100,000–200,000 Ukrainian IT workers relocated to EU countries, per **USAID Digital Economy of Ukraine** reports from 2023–2024), but it also triggered a consolidation effect: the companies with physical resilience and mission-critical products kept hiring domestically while outsourcers froze or offshored. Ajax, whose security systems saw demand spikes in the European market precisely because of the war context, was positioned to absorb talent that outsourcers shed.

By Summer 2026, according to **DOU's own Top-50 analysis**, EPAM Ukraine still leads at approximately 8,000+ specialists, SoftServe holds second at roughly 6,500–7,000, and Ajax has now claimed third at 5,589 — ahead of GlobalLogic for the first time. This is not a marginal gap. It represents years of compounded trajectory divergence.

There is a secondary data point worth noting: Ajax's **1,452 technical specialists** represent roughly 26% of its total workforce. Compare this to a pure-play outsourcer where 70–80% of headcount is billable technical staff. Ajax's lower technical ratio reflects the full operational stack required to ship a hardware product — manufacturing coordination, supply chain, customer success, compliance — but it also means the *technical hiring velocity* is more meaningful than raw headcount growth might suggest. Growing technical staff by 225 in six months inside a hardware-software company means real product capability expansion, not just service delivery scaling.

For context, **Stack Overflow's 2025 Developer Survey** (the most recent comprehensive global benchmark) noted that embedded and hardware-adjacent engineering roles are among the hardest to fill globally, with median time-to-hire exceeding 90 days for senior positions. Ajax filling 225+ such roles in 6 months — in a wartime labor market — is operationally notable.

The broader question for the Ukrainian tech ecosystem is whether this signals a durable structural shift toward product companies dominating the talent rankings, or whether it is Ajax-specific. Given that MacPaw, Grammarly (Kyiv-founded), and Genesis portfolio companies have all maintained or grown Ukrainian engineering teams through 2025–2026, the evidence leans toward structural. Product companies with global revenue and domestic engineering roots are proving more resilient than client-dependent outsourcers in the current environment.
